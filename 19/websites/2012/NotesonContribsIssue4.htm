Notes on Contributors

Nicola Bown is senior lecturer in Victorian Literature at Birkbeck College. She is the author of Fairies in Nineteenth-Century Art and Literature (Cambridge 2001) and co-editor of The Victorian Supernatural (Cambridge 2003) and has two sons. Her current research concerns the space between life and death, and the way this was represented in Victorian culture

Emma Mason is a lecturer in the Department of English and Comparative Literary Studies at the University of Warwick. She is the author of Women Poets of the Nineteenth Century (2006) and, with Mark Knight, Nineteenth-Century Religion and Literature: an introduction (2006).

Kirstie Blair is a lecturer in English Literature at the University of Glasgow. Her primary research interests are in Victorian poetry and Victorian religion, both separately and together. She has published widely in these fields. Recent publications include a special edition of Victorian Poetry on Tractarian poets, co-edited with Emma Mason, two articles on spasmodic poetry, and the monograph Victorian Poetry and the Culture of the Heart (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 2006).

Sally Ledger is Professor of Nineteenth-Century Literature at Birkbeck, University of London. Her most recent book is Dickens and the Popular Radical Imagination (Cambridge University Press, 2007); previous studies have included The New Woman: Fiction and Feminism at the Fin de Siecle (Manchester University Press, 1997) and Henrik Ibsen (Northcote House, 1999; 2nd edition 2007). Her current project is an anthology of 45 critical essays on Dickens for Cambridge University Press, called Dickens in Context.

Heather Tilley is a third-year PhD student at Birkbeck, University of London, writing a thesis on cultural representations of blindness in the first half of the nineteenth century. Her research focuses on the relationship between tropes of blindness and the figure of the writer. Forthcoming publications include an article on dramatic representations of blindness in a special edition of the journal Nineteenth Century Theatre and Film , from the Victorian Dramas conference (Worcester, 2007). Her wider research interests include the relationship between Romanticism and the Victorians, literary theory and material culture, gender and vision, nineteenth-century biography and autobiography studies.

Sonia Solicari studied english literature, nineteenth-century studies, and museology before joining the Victoria and Albert Museum in 2002 as an Assistant Curator in the Word & Image Department. Previous research has centred around Victorian narrative painting, contemporary notions of taste, and the relationship between art, literature and mass culture. She is currently Curator of nineteenth-century ceramics and glass, working on a major redisplay of the V&A's ceramics collection. Her research interests include nineteenth-century historicism, Victorian art pottery and the display of ceramics in the domestic interior.

Marie Banfield studied for her first degree in English and American Literature at the University of Kent where she became interested in the interrelations between literature, science and philosophy. Her second degree was taken a number of years later through the Open University, where her research focussed on the contribution of scientific ideas to the poetry of Tennyson and Hopkins. At present she is a member of Birkbeck College, University of London, completing a PhD thesis on the novels and poetry of George Meredith in their contemporary intellectual and scientific context.
