Notes on Contributors
Regenia Gagnier is the Professor of English, Director of Research, and Director of the Centre for Victorian Studies at the University of Exeter. Her specialisms include nineteenth century British culture and society, social theory, world literatures, and interdisciplinary studies. Her most recent books are The Insatiability of Human Wants: Economics and Aesthetics in Market Society (Chicago, 2000) and a special issue (with Angelique Richardson) of Victorian Literature and Culture on "Victorian Boundaries" (2004).
Rohan McWilliam is Senior Lecturer in History at Anglia Polytechnic University, Cambridge. He is the author of Popular Politics in Nineteenth-Century England (Routledge, 1998) and has co-edited The Victorian Studies Reader with Kelly Boyd (forthcoming from Routledge). His book on The Tichborne Claimant will be published by Hambledon in 2006.

Patrizia Di Bello lectures on the History and Theory of Photography in the School of History of Art, Film and Visual Media at Birkbeck College, University of London. She is currently working on a book on Victorian women's albums, and researching the interactions between photography and tactility.

 

Michael Allis is Senior Tutor in Postgraduate Studies at the Royal Academy of Music, London. His recent publications include Parry's Creative Process (Ashgate, 2003), articles in Music and Letters and the Journal of Musicological Research on Elgar and narrative, and a chapter on musical settings of Tennyson (‘Musical Reactions to Tennyson: Reformulating Musical Imagery in “The Lotos-Eaters”') in Phyllis Weliver (ed.), The Figure of Music in Nineteenth-Century British Poetry (Ashgate 2005). Current projects include aspects of British Liszt reception, tempo in Wagner, and an exploration of parallels between British music and literature in the long nineteenth century, provisionally entitled British Music and Literary Context.

James Mussell is a part-time postdoctoral reseach associate on ncse.  His work explores the place of science within late nineteenth-century print culture, and focuses especially on the role that print plays in mediating events across space and time.  He also currently teaches at Kings College London and Birkbeck College and is preparing a monograph, Movable Types: the Times and Spaces of Science in the Late Nineteenth-Century Periodical Press, which considers the material culture of science, print, and reading.
Suzanne Paylor is a full-time researcher on the ncse project. Her research interests include the history of popular print culture and the history of science communication in popular media. Recent and forthcoming publications include articles on Edward B. Aveling and the readership for science in the early nineteenth century.
Anne Humpherys is a Professor of English at Lehman College and the Graduate Center, City University of New York. She is the author of Travels in the Poor Man's Country: The Work of Henry Mayhew and many articles and chapters on Victorian literature, periodicals, and culture. She was a Leverhulme Visiting Professor at Birkbeck in 2005.
