<p>A quick heads-up to flag the following conference, taking place on the 17th May at the University of Sussex. Excellent exciting line-up!</p>
<p><a href="https://www.martineve.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Modernist-intimacies-poster-3.jpg"><img src="https://www.martineve.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Modernist-intimacies-poster-3-723x1024.jpg" alt="Modernist intimacies poster" width="723" height="1024" class="alignnone size-large wp-image-2647" /></a></p>
<p>Modernist Intimacies: Panel Schedule.<br />
9.00-9.30: Registration and coffee [Jubilee 155].<br />
9:30-9:45: Conference Welcome: Ruth Charnock</p>
<p>9.45-11.00: Affective reading/reflexive reading [Jubilee 155].<br />
Martin Ryle [University of Sussex]: ‘New intimacies with the self: Joycean monologue and self-fashioning.’<br />
Jen Cooke [Loughborough University]: ‘When Women Read, or, Scenes of Angry Modernist Learning.’<br />
Jonathan Foltz [Boston University]: ‘Formalisms of the Self: Vernon Lee and the Physiology of Reading.’<br />
Chair: Ruth Charnock [University of Sussex]</p>
<p>Tactile objects [Jubilee 143].<br />
Jo Winning [Birkbeck College]: ‘Love and the Made Object.’<br />
Hannah Wen-Shan Shieh [University of Sussex]: ‘Katherine Mansfield’s Art of Changing Masks.’<br />
Rosie Langridge [Queen Mary]: ‘Forsterian Kisses.’<br />
Chair: Pam Thurschwell [University of Sussex]</p>
<p>11.00-11.30: Break</p>
<p>11.30-12.45: Perilous Fashionings [Jubilee 155].<br />
Santanu Das [KCL]: ‘Perilous intimacies: wounding, writing and feeling in early 20th century literature.’<br />
Barry Sheils [University of Swansea]: ‘Wit and Environs: the problems of male intimacy in Wilde and Joyce.’<br />
Benjamin Poore [Queen Mary]: ‘Masud Khan’s Ulysses: Exile, Race thinking, and modernist self-fashioning.’</p>
<p>Chair: Martin Ryle [University of Sussex]</p>
<p>12.45-1.45: Lunch</p>
<p>1.45-3.00: Missed Connections and Vile Bodies [Jubilee 155].<br />
Ben Clarke [University of North Carolina, Greensboro]: ‘Public faces in private spaces: space and intimacy in Evelyn Waugh’s Vile Bodies.’<br />
Hugh Stevens [UCL]: ‘Mr Forster visits Mr and Mrs Lawrence.’<br />
Ruth Charnock [University of Sussex] Feeling Modernist: Anais Nin and D. H. Lawrence<br />
Chair: Alistair Davies [University of Sussex]</p>
<p>Intimate (Dis)locations [Jubilee 143].<br />
Rebecca Thorndike-Breeze [Northeastern University]: ‘Intimacy in Isolation and the Amplitude of Reality: Virginia Woolf’s Tense Intimacies in The Voyage Out, Mrs. Dalloway and The Waves.’<br />
Karina Jakubowicz [UCL]: ‘'"Every bed she leaves, without caring”: Beds, Identity, and the Aesthetics of Intimacy in Djuna Barnes’s Nightwood.’<br />
Pru Chamberlain [Royal Holloway]: ‘Mina Loy’s Hazardous Incognito.’<br />
Chair: Sara Crangle [University of Sussex]</p>
<p>3.00-3.15: Break</p>
<p>3.15-4.30: Close ups, bad feelings [Jubilee 155].<br />
Geoff Gilbert [American University of Paris]: ‘Do you know how you feel: Shame and the Persistence of Modernism.’<br />
Francesco Ventrella [University of Sussex]: ‘Connoisseurial Intimacies: Homoeroticism and Empathic Cock-Tease.’<br />
Charles Burnetts [University of Western Ontario]: ‘Béla Balázs and Sensibility.’<br />
Chair: J. D. Rhodes [University of Sussex]</p>
<p>4.30-5.45: Keynote address [Jubilee 155].<br />
Denis Flannery [University of Leeds]: ‘How to Live Together; or, Modernist Intimacy in 1897.’<br />
Chair: Pam Thurschwell [University of Sussex]</p>
<p>5.45-6.45: *Conference wrap-up drinks: IDS bar</p>
